  Part II: at least two cases where ALPHA-BETA evaluates fewer positions than MINIMAX,
           with the same MINIMAX estimate and the same move.
  Part IV: at least two board positions where the improved static estimation selects
           a different move than the original one, with a reason for preferring it.

Each case shows the exact command and the three lines the submitted program printed.
Input files, in the assets/ folder, are the two positions drawn in the Morris Variant handout:
  assets/handout_position_1.txt = xxxxxBxWWWWWBBBBxxxxxxx
  assets/handout_position_2.txt = WxWWxWWWWWBBBBBBBBxxxxx
Depth counts individual turns (plies). "Positions evaluated" counts calls to the static estimator.


PART II: ALPHA-BETA EVALUATES FEWER POSITIONS THAN MINIMAX

Case 1 (opening, depth 3)
Input board: xxxxxBxWWWWWBBBBxxxxxxx

MINIMAX:
  $ python3 MiniMaxOpening.py assets/handout_position_1.txt board2.txt 3
  Board Position: xxxxxxxWWWWWBBBBWxxxxxx
  Positions evaluated by static estimation: 3778
  MINIMAX estimate: 2

ALPHA-BETA:
  $ python3 ABOpening.py assets/handout_position_1.txt board2.txt 3
  Board Position: xxxxxxxWWWWWBBBBWxxxxxx
  Positions evaluated by static estimation: 481
  MINIMAX estimate: 2

Selected move: White places a piece at e4 and removes the Black piece at f1
Same move and same MINIMAX estimate (2). Alpha-beta evaluated 481 positions instead of 3778 (87% fewer).
Alpha-beta stops exploring a branch once it cannot change the decision above it.
Both searches try moves in the same order and keep the first move on a tie.

PART IV: IMPROVED STATIC ESTIMATION SELECTS A DIFFERENT MOVE
Scores from the two estimators use different scales. We compare the chosen moves, not the numbers.

Case 1 (opening, depth 2)
Input board: WxWWxWWWWWBBBBBBBBxxxxx

ORIGINAL ESTIMATION:
  $ python3 MiniMaxOpening.py assets/handout_position_2.txt board2.txt 2
  Board Position: WWWWxWWWWWBBBBBBBxxxxxx
  Positions evaluated by static estimation: 76
  MINIMAX estimate: 1
  Move: White places a piece at d0 and removes the Black piece at b5

IMPROVED ESTIMATION:
  $ python3 MiniMaxOpeningImproved.py assets/handout_position_2.txt board2.txt 2
  Board Position: WxWWxWWWWWBBBBBBBxxxWxx
  Positions evaluated by static estimation: 76
  MINIMAX estimate: 203
  Move: White places a piece at a6 and removes the Black piece at b5

Feature differences after each move (White minus Black):
  original move: {'pieces': 2, 'mills': 1, 'threats': 3, 'forks': 0, 'mobility': 0, 'blocked': 2, 'connections': 5}
  improved move: {'pieces': 2, 'mills': 1, 'threats': 3, 'forks': 0, 'mobility': 2, 'blocked': 1, 'connections': 7}

Why we prefer the improved move: Both moves close a mill and remove the same Black piece, so the original function gives them the same score and keeps the first one, d0. The improved function prefers a6, which lies on three mill lines, over d0, which lies on one. After the move, White's connection advantage is 7 instead of 5, White has 2 more free neighboring squares than Black instead of an equal number, and one blocked piece more than Black instead of two. The search also checked Black's reply at depth 2.

Case 2 (midgame, depth 2)
Input board: WxWWxWWWWWBBBBBBBBxxxxx

ORIGINAL ESTIMATION:
  $ python3 MiniMaxGame.py assets/handout_position_2.txt board2.txt 2
  Board Position: WxWWxWWWxWBBBBBBBBxxWxx
  Positions evaluated by static estimation: 31
  MINIMAX estimate: -11
  Move: White moves a3 to a6

IMPROVED ESTIMATION:
  $ python3 MiniMaxGameImproved.py assets/handout_position_2.txt board2.txt 2
  Board Position: WxWxWWWWWWBBBBBBBBxxxxx
  Positions evaluated by static estimation: 31
  MINIMAX estimate: -50
  Move: White moves b1 to d1

Feature differences after each move (White minus Black):
  original move: {'pieces': 0, 'mills': 0, 'threats': 1, 'forks': 0, 'mobility': 3, 'blocked': -1, 'connections': 2}
  improved move: {'pieces': 0, 'mills': -1, 'threats': 2, 'forks': 0, 'mobility': 2, 'blocked': -1, 'connections': -1}

Why we prefer the improved move: It slides b1 to d1. From d1 the piece can slide on to d0 and close a0-d0-g0, and a0 or b3 can slide into b1 and close b1-d1-f1, so White has two different mill-closing squares instead of one: the threat difference becomes +2 instead of +1. Black would need two moves to cover both. The price is that White's a0-b1-c2 mill is open for now (mills -1 instead of 0) and its mobility lead is +2 instead of +3. The original move, a3 to a6, blocks Black's c4-b5-a6 line and leaves Black 5 moves instead of 6, which is the only thing besides material that the original midgame function measures.
